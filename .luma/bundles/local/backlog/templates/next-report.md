# What's next template

**Where the work stands, then an opinion.** Copy the shape, not this file.
Listings inside it follow [[listing]].

```
### Last touched

<one line: which record, roughly when, and what it was>

### Under way

<a listing --- in_progress>

### Queued

<a listing --- todo>

### Worth preparing

<up to three, marked, with a clause each on why>

---

### Risks

<what you noticed that nobody asked about --- or omit the section>

### Where to start

1. **<the thing>** — <one clause of why>
2. **<the thing>** — <one clause of why>
```

**The rule separates reading from opinion.** Above it, what the corpus says.
Below it, yours. Same division as the record view, for the same reason.

**Last touched is one line.** Somebody returning after a break wants their place
back before they want advice. Say when nothing has been touched recently, and
how long --- a cold backlog and a hot one call for different answers.

**Under way and queued are shown even when empty**, with their commands. Two
empty listings *are* the finding: nothing is selected, and no amount of reading
further changes it.

**Worth preparing appears only when there is nothing to do**, or when what is
queued is thin. **Three at most**, and never offered as work --- it has not
crossed the second gate, and offering it mistakes a pile for a queue.

**Where nothing is close, say so and name one or two anyway** --- not as
recommendations but as a gauge, so somebody can see how far preparation is from
producing anything.

**Risks is omitted when there are none.** A manufactured concern costs more than
the section is worth and teaches a reader to skip it.

**Where to start is one to five, ranked, a clause each.** Not a plan --- a
shortlist somebody can act on without reading twice. Say what would change the
order when something obvious would, usually a decision nobody has made.
