## ▶ 2026-09-20

The vetting round taught three things about the method itself. **Three models given identical briefs in identical isolation reached three different conclusions** --- one per option --- which is the clearest evidence yet that this method surfaces disagreement rather than manufacturing consensus, and that a three-way split is itself a finding: the evidence does not determine the design. **Each of the three built a fourth design and rejected its own**, all failing the same way, which is stronger evidence about the design space than any of the three options received. And **the briefs' numbers were attacked hardest, not its reasoning** --- three of five headline measurements turned out to be artifacts, including one that depended on a per-machine git config. **So the checklist needs a sixth question: does every number in the brief say what produced it?** A measurement without its method is indistinguishable from an assertion, and a reader cannot attack what it cannot see.

## ▶ 2026-09-18

A sixth checklist class, found the hard way on WORK-0096: **does the brief carry the output conventions the result has to satisfy?** The isolated agents named a competing project six times across two records, which CLAUDE.md forbids --- and they could not have known, because they held the problem brief and nothing else. Isolation from our thinking is also isolation from our conventions. The brief must withhold **what we think** while carrying **how we write**, and those are easy to conflate because both live in the same documents. This is the mirror of the anchoring problem and it points the other way, which is why five passes of checking for leaks never found it.
